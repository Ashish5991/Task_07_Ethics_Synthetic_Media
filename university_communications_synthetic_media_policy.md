# University Communications Office Policy on Synthetic Media

**Policy owner:** Office of University Communications  
**Applies to:** Employees, student employees, contractors, and agencies producing media for the Office of University Communications  
**Effective date:** Draft for adoption  
**Review cycle:** Annual and after any material incident

## 1. Purpose and Scope

This policy governs media that is generated, materially altered, or presented by an artificial-intelligence system in a way that could cause a reasonable audience to believe a person spoke, appeared, acted, or endorsed a message when they did not. It covers audio, video, images, avatars, voice clones, and synthetic representations used in university communications.

The policy applies to content published on university-controlled websites, social accounts, email, advertising, event displays, and outreach channels. It also applies to draft content produced by contractors before publication.

This policy does not prohibit ordinary editing, accessibility captions, color correction, noise reduction, or clearly nonrepresentational illustrations. When uncertain, staff must request review under Section 6 before production.

## 2. Core Rule

The office may use synthetic media only when the use has a documented public-interest or accessibility benefit, does not create a misleading impression of a real person's speech or endorsement, and satisfies every requirement in this policy. Faster production, lower cost, or novelty alone is not sufficient justification.

## 3. Permitted Uses

The following uses may be permitted after approval:

- A synthetic narrator with no resemblance to an identifiable person for accessibility-oriented summaries, instructional material, or routine informational content.
- Clearly labeled synthetic illustrations or avatars that do not depict an identifiable person and are not presented as documentary evidence.
- Translation or dubbing of a recorded speaker's own message when the speaker gives written, project-specific consent and the output is labeled as AI-generated or AI-assisted.
- Restoration or enhancement of university-owned historical media when the work does not invent speech, actions, or events and the alteration is disclosed.
- Internal prototypes that are not distributed outside the review team and are deleted or retained according to the project record.

Permitted use is not automatic. Every project must meet the review, consent, disclosure, and recordkeeping requirements below.

## 4. Prohibited Uses

The office must not:

- Clone, simulate, or materially alter an identifiable person's voice, likeness, or mannerisms without their written, project-specific consent.
- Create or distribute synthetic media that attributes a statement, action, endorsement, expertise, or emotion to a real person who did not provide it.
- Use synthetic media in emergency alerts, safety instructions, legal notices, disciplinary communications, financial aid decisions, health communications, or any message where identity and authenticity are essential to safety or legal effect.
- Produce content intended to deceive, evade accountability, manipulate a political position, target minors with personalized persuasion, or impersonate a student, employee, alumnus, donor, public official, or member of the public.
- Create sexually explicit, humiliating, discriminatory, harassing, or retaliatory synthetic content.
- Use a synthetic representation of a deceased person without written authorization from the person or authorized estate representative and approval from University Counsel. The office should decline the project if the proposed use is promotional, controversial, or likely to confuse audiences.
- Publish an AI-generated factual claim unless the claim has been reviewed and verified by the responsible content owner using reliable source material.

No disclosure, watermark, consent form, or contractual term overrides these prohibitions.

## 5. Consent Workflow

Before creating content that uses an identifiable person's voice, likeness, or distinctive characteristics, the project owner must obtain written consent that states:

- The person represented and the specific source material to be used.
- The intended message, channels, audience, territory, and publication period.
- Whether synthesis, translation, dubbing, or alteration will occur.
- The approved final script or a description of allowable variations.
- How the synthetic nature of the content will be disclosed.
- The contact for questions and the process for withdrawing consent before publication.

Consent expires at the end of the stated publication period and is not transferable to a new project, campaign, or tool. A person may withdraw consent before publication without penalty. After publication, the office will remove or stop distributing the content within five business days where technically and legally feasible, and will document any copies it cannot control. Consent records must be stored with the project record for three years after publication.

## 6. Review and Approval

Before production, the project owner must submit a request to the Synthetic Media Review Group, consisting of a communications leader, accessibility representative, privacy or legal representative, and the relevant content owner. The group must determine:

- The purpose and why a nonsynthetic alternative is insufficient.
- Whether an identifiable person, sensitive subject, or vulnerable audience is involved.
- Whether the proposed content is permitted and has valid consent.
- How factual statements will be verified.
- The disclosure, provenance, publication, retention, and incident-response plan.

Approval must be in writing before a contractor or employee generates a publishable artifact. Projects involving a real person's synthetic voice or likeness require final approval after the completed output is reviewed. The Review Group may require changes, restrict distribution, or refuse a project when the residual risk exceeds the anticipated benefit.

## 7. Disclosure Standards

All published synthetic media must use clear, plain-language disclosure appropriate to the format:

- Audio must include a spoken notice at the beginning: "This audio was generated or altered with AI."
- Video must display a readable on-screen notice at the start and include the same disclosure in the caption or description.
- Images must include a disclosure in alt-adjacent text, caption, or surrounding page text; social posts must include it in the post text.
- Any synthetic use of an identifiable person must state whether the person's contribution was recorded, translated, dubbed, or synthesized.

The disclosure must not be hidden in terms of use, a hashtag alone, or a page that the audience is unlikely to see. For content likely to be clipped or shared, the project owner must include a short disclosure in the media itself and retain an official source URL in the description where possible.

## 8. Provenance, Records, and Vendor Requirements

When the production tool supports Content Credentials or equivalent provenance metadata, the office must enable it and preserve it in the original published file. Staff must not claim that provenance metadata makes content true or prevents alteration.

For every approved project, the project owner must retain:

- The approval record, consent record if applicable, final script, sources used to verify factual claims, and final published files.
- Tool and version, material prompts or settings, date of generation, editor, and disclosure text.
- The publication URLs, intended channels, and any available credential or watermark information.

Contracts with agencies and vendors must require compliance with this policy, prohibit use of university inputs for unrelated model training without written authorization, require prompt notification of a security or privacy incident, and require delivery of project records.

## 9. Publication and Audience Protections

Synthetic media may not be the sole channel for a message that requires verification, action, consent, or immediate safety response. Official notices must use established authenticated channels and identify a human office or contact point.

Automated personalized synthetic outreach requires additional Review Group approval. The request must identify the audience, data fields used, expected volume, frequency limit, opt-out process, and human escalation contact. It may not imply an individual relationship or personal endorsement that does not exist.

## 10. Incident Response

Anyone who discovers suspected misuse, misattribution, loss of disclosure, or unauthorized synthetic content associated with the university must report it to the Office of University Communications and the information-security incident channel immediately.

Within one business day, the office will:

- Preserve the relevant files, publication records, screenshots, and links.
- Assess whether the content was authorized, altered, or falsely attributed.
- Pause distribution of affected university-controlled content when warranted.
- Notify the represented person, relevant university leaders, and University Counsel when identity, safety, privacy, or legal risk is involved.
- Publish a correction or verification notice through authenticated channels when a public audience may be misled.

The Review Group will document the incident, identify corrective actions, and determine whether the policy, vendor contract, training, or approval process requires revision.

## 11. Training, Enforcement, and Audit

Covered staff must complete annual training on this policy, consent, disclosure, and reporting. The policy owner will maintain an approval register and conduct a quarterly sample audit of published synthetic-media projects for required disclosure, project records, and approvals.

Failure to comply may result in removal of content, loss of publishing access, contract remedies, or disciplinary action under applicable university policy. Intentional deception, nonconsensual impersonation, or retaliation against someone who reports a concern must be escalated to the appropriate university office.

## 12. Policy Limitations and Residual Risk

This policy cannot stop a bad-faith actor from generating material outside university systems, stripping a disclosure from a clip, or falsely claiming that an authentic university message is synthetic. It cannot ensure that platforms retain labels or provenance metadata after upload, conversion, or reposting. It also cannot make automated detection reliable enough to decide authenticity without human verification.

The policy depends on staff recognizing synthetic-media projects and seeking review. A well-intentioned employee may classify an alteration as routine editing, use a consumer tool without realizing that it creates a synthetic likeness, or share a draft outside the approved workflow. Training and audits reduce these risks but cannot eliminate them.

Consent has limits as well. A person may agree to one audience and later find that the content reaches a different context. Removal cannot erase downloaded copies. For this reason, the office should use synthetic representations of identifiable people only when the benefit is clear and a less risky alternative is inadequate.

Finally, the policy cannot solve the broader erosion of trust caused by widespread synthetic media. Its realistic goal is narrower: make the university's own conduct legible, reduce avoidable harms, preserve records that support verification, and provide a fast response when something goes wrong. The office must review the policy annually because tools, platform practices, law, and audience expectations will change.
