# Task 07: Ethics of Synthetic Representation

## Project Description

This project examines the ethical implications of synthetic media and translates that analysis into a practical governance policy. It begins with a synthetic audio artifact produced for Task 6 and asks what changes when the same capability is used with false content, without consent, without disclosure, or at larger scale.

The project has two deliverables:

- `ethical_analysis.md`: Phase A analysis grounded in the Task 6 artifact, four thought experiments, and a review of mitigation limits.
- `university_communications_synthetic_media_policy.md`: Phase B policy for a university communications office, followed by an honest limitations and residual-risk assessment.

## Organizational Context

The policy is written for a university communications office that produces public-facing admissions, alumni, research, event, and emergency communications. This context was chosen because a university depends on institutional trust, reaches audiences with unequal technical literacy, and has regular access to the voices and likenesses of students, faculty, staff, and visitors. Synthetic media may make routine work faster, but it can also create false impressions of endorsement, expertise, or official institutional speech.

## Relationship to Task 6

This analysis builds on the [Task 6 synthetic-media work](../Task_06_Deep_Fake/), specifically the two clearly labeled, synthetic text-to-speech audio artifacts and their process, evaluation, and provenance logs. Task 6 showed that a free local text-to-speech workflow can make intelligible narration quickly while leaving audible synthetic cues and no embedded C2PA credentials in the exported WAV files.

## Reflection

The most surprising part of the Task 6 to Task 7 transition was that obvious synthetic quality does not remove the ethical problem. Even a flat, clearly artificial voice can borrow the authority of institutional speech. The risk becomes much larger when an audience encounters a short excerpt without its original disclosure, source context, or supporting documentation.
